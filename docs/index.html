The problem

Your testing tools arrive after
the damage is done

Playwright, Cypress, JMeter, K6 — the entire testing industry operates where defects already exist. But when a user story says "the link expires quickly" or "the password must be secure", no automation framework can save you. The defect was born before the first commit.

🕳️

Ambiguity becomes rework

Untestable requirements force developers to guess. Every wrong guess is a bug in QA, a difficult conversation with the client, and a sprint that slips.

🤝

"Known bugs" ship under pressure

When deadlines hit and nobody can prove what was actually agreed, defects get waved through to UAT as "known issues" — and quality erodes silently, decision by decision.

🔍

Nobody can answer "was this specified?"

Without traceability from business rule to test case, every defect triage becomes archaeology — and every scope discussion becomes a negotiation without evidence.

The solution

From ambiguous story to

FAQ

Fair questions, straight answers

Does the AI invent requirements?

No — it's forbidden by design. Anything absent from the source document is flagged as NOT SPECIFIED, and every value the engine proposes (like "link expires in 30 minutes") is explicitly marked [PROPOSED] for business confirmation. The AI decides what requires semantic judgment; deterministic code computes every score, ID and decision rule.

How is the score not just an AI opinion?

The RHI is weighted arithmetic over 29 numbered rules (Critical=5, High=3, Medium=2, Low=1). Each verdict cites evidence from the text. The calculation is published in every certificate — 22/93 points is a fact you can recompute, not a vibe.

What standards does the catalog encode?

ISTQB CTFL v4, ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148 (requirements engineering), ISO/IEC/IEEE 29119 (test documentation), IEEE 1028 (formal reviews) and OWASP Top 10:2021. The catalog is a versioned YAML file — extend it, and the engine enforces your rules too.

Does this replace analysts or QA?

The opposite. Analysts get their stories corrected with justification before the client meeting. QA gets test assets on day one. Humans keep the signatures: analyst approves the normalization, client approves the proposed values. NEXUS prepares evidence; people decide.

Is it really open source? Can I run it?

Yes. The engine, the rules catalog, the schemas and a fully verified run are public on GitHub. Clone it, add an Anthropic API key, and certify your first user story in minutes — offline evals included so you can validate the engine before spending a cent.
